2. The Concept in Brief

The concept is based on the awareness of the meaning and role of the relationship between two inseparable constituent sides of any element of reality (object, process, phenomenon): content and form.

Key idea: at the basis of all real processes lies the relation {content/form}, where content is the active, mobile side, and form is the passive, retaining side. Content strives to escape the power of form, but is realized only through form. This generates an endless spiral-cyclic movement of development.

Positioning of the concept:

  1. A unified (integrative) foundation for fragmentary scientific knowledge and separately existing sciences.
  2. A universal language for interdisciplinary communication.
  3. An alternative to hot wars and acute conflicts on Earth.

3. The Four Articles and Their Theses

Article 1. Concept of Development: The Fundamental Principle

Core — the universal principle of self-development (self-organization) of reality.

"The development of content proceeds in the direction of exhaustive expression (coverage, absorption, assimilation, comprehension...) by the developing content of the very same developing content."

Consequences:

  • The external manifestation of the process @ is the renewal of the form of expression of accumulating content by raising the "level" (tier) of the form.
  • The movement of content is realized through non-repeating-in-detail forms of different levels of representation.
  • All real processes have a spiral-cyclic character. The world and all its elements are spiral-shaped: man, dog, ant, living cell, molecule, atom, electron, photon, galaxy, Universe — a spiral.
  • The vector of universal movement: "everywhere and always there is a total multi-level movement toward achieving the highest level of pithiness (content-richness)".
  • The principle @ gives an idea of the unidirectionality and multi-levelness of development.

Article 2. Nature and Sense of the World Constants

Thesis: the world "constants" (π, e, φ) are not numbers-constants, but external forms of manifestation of internal connections of reality, limiting ratios of constituent sides, "bridges" between them.

  • World "constants" are external forms of manifestation of internal connections and relations of constituent sides of reality; "bridges" between them.
  • Examples of remarkable limits π, φ, e testify to the principal feasibility of quantitative expression of irrational sides of reality through rational ones.
  • The irrationality of numbers π, φ, e indicates the principal unattainability of exhaustive expression of the irrational side through the rational one, but the possibility of arbitrarily close approximation.
  • These numbers are not "constants" but forms of infinite processes of transition from one constituent side of reality to another.
  • World "constants" are "boundary pillars" along the perimeter of the boundary of the most studied by man subject (physical) level of reality.

Article 3. The Universal Criterion of Truth

Thesis (based on V.I. Lenin's "Materialism and Empirio-criticism"):

The criterion of truth (of the truthfulness of forms of developing reality) is not practice, but the pithiness (content-richness) of these forms. Practice is only a method or procedure for evaluating the completeness ("quantity") of the pithiness of forms of reality — a particular case of a more developed technology of establishing truth, namely interpretation.
  • The criterion of truth allows quantitatively evaluating the significance of forms of reality and ranking them.
  • There are no two or more equally pithy truths regarding one and the same question under investigation.
  • The notion of "reflection" ("copy", "mapping") of reality serves as a limitation or barrier on the path of understanding the essence of the processes of development and cognition.
  • Objective reality is the movement and change of forms in the direction of possessing the highest pithiness.

Article 4. The Unlearned Lessons of Dialectics

Thesis: the lessons of dialectics from V.I. Lenin. Any work of Lenin is permeated with the spirit of dialectics; any of his thoughts is a chain of conceptual forms maximally filled with objective content.

The scheme of productive ("triadic") development of an idea: thesis — antithesis — synthesis; the unity of the historical and the logical.

The algorithm of dialectical analysis:

  1. Composing correct relations of directly interacting constituent sides in the forms under investigation (objects, processes, phenomena). One must identify and show the directly interacting (most essential at the moment) opposing, competing, contending constituent sides.
  2. Determining and arranging priorities in the found relations, i.e., identifying the active and passive constituent sides in the composed relations.
  3. Determining the probable direction of change (transformation), movement, development of the constituent components (i.e., the probable change of the ratio or balance of components) within the relations formed from them, and consequently the direction of variability of the relations themselves as a whole (taking into account the impacts and influence of surrounding forms on these relations).
  4. Forecasting (anticipating) development of the relations under investigation, i.e., determining the prospects of modification of the forms under investigation (objects, processes, phenomena), as well as the prospects of emergence of new forms.

When moving to the next stage of research or the next form of reality under investigation, points 1–4 are repeated in the same volume.
